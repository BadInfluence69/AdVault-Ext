AdVault

Blocks the request, then hides the leftovers.

Always on, no switch: ad & malvertising request blocking (57,000 rules) and, on YouTube, player recovery if the picture ever comes up black. Both work everywhere and there is no reason to turn them off, so they are not options.

Page ads

23,000 EasyList element rules across 7,700 sites, matched to the page as it loads.

Video players

Sends a 4-character hash prefix of the video id to sponsor.ajay.app.

DVR playback only. Reads the player’s own break markers, or breaks you mark with Alt+M. Nothing is guessed from the picture.

How this behaves on YouTube

YouTube is the hard case, so here is exactly what happens — no guessing which switch to try.

  • Skippable ads are handled by Click “Skip”: AdVault mutes the ad and clicks Skip the instant the button appears.
  • Non-skippable ads cannot be clicked away, so AdVault seeks through the part of the ad that has already downloaded, over and over, until it runs out. On a decent connection a 30-second ad is gone in about two seconds. On a slow one it is limited by the download itself — nothing can skip data that has not arrived yet.
  • Nothing seeks inside a YouTube ad. That is what used to black the player out, so it cannot happen any more.
  • Sponsor segments inside the video are a different thing from ads — those are SponsorBlock, and they come from the community, not from YouTube.
  • No rule here blocks a request YouTube's own player makes, so its anti-adblock has nothing of ours to notice. Third-party ad hosts are still blocked everywhere, YouTube included.
  • If the player ever comes up black, recovery clears it on its own and says what it found in the console (filter for [AdVault]). You should not have to touch a switch.

Page ads on youtube.com — feed, sidebar, masthead — are the Hide page ads switch, the same as any other site. uBO site rules deliberately does not run on YouTube; YouTube is handled by the rules above instead.
